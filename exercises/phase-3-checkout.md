# Phase III Checkout — HF/NVIS Reference Study

## Mission
Perform a controlled comparison of:
1. low horizontal dipole;
2. center-supported inverted-V;
3. crossed/sloping reference architecture.

## Frequencies
At minimum: 5, 7, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35 MHz.

## Common assumptions
Use identical ground model, conductivity, relative permittivity, conductor assumptions, normalization, and angular sampling wherever comparison requires them. Document exceptions.

## Required outputs
For every architecture/frequency:
- R and X
- VSWR with stated reference impedance
- current-distribution observations
- zenith gain
- gain at 60°, 70°, 80°
- peak elevation
- high-elevation azimuth variation

Where supported, also report radiation efficiency, realized gain, and ground/material loss.

## Height
Report important heights in both meters and wavelengths.

## Ground sensitivity
At a representative frequency compare multiple finite-ground cases with numerical conductivity and relative permittivity.

## Convergence
Perform convergence checks on at least one representative case per architecture.

## Interpretation
Answer:
1. Where is high-angle behavior strongest across the sampled band?
2. When do patterns become multi-lobed?
3. How do currents explain those changes?
4. Which results are most ground-sensitive?
5. Which are height-sensitive?
6. Where is matching difficult?
7. Does good matching correspond to useful high-angle radiation?
8. What appears to be caused by coupling?
9. What feed/common-mode effects remain inadequately represented?
10. What must Phase IV investigate?

Do not declare an overall "best antenna." Identify tradeoffs and evidence needs.

## Deliverable
Models, run records, tables, comparable plots, convergence evidence, assumptions, limitations, and a Phase IV test plan.
