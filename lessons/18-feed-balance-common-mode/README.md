# Lesson 18 — Feed, Balance, and Common-Mode Effects

**Phase:** HF/NVIS Engineering  
**Target time:** 90 minutes

## Objective
Recognize when the feed system becomes part of the antenna.

## Theory
Understand balanced versus unbalanced structures, differential current, common-mode current, balun/choke purpose, feed-line radiation, and limitations of simplified NEC feed models.

A good 50-ohm reading does not prove common-mode current is absent.

## Lab A — ideal reference
Start with an ideal balanced source. Record impedance, currents, and pattern.

## Lab B — representative feed conductor
Where appropriate, add a simplified conductive feed/support path. Compare current on it, feed impedance, and pattern. Do not claim a simple wire exactly represents coax.

## Lab C — routing sensitivity
Alter the representative feed route. If pattern or impedance changes, the feed cannot be treated as electromagnetically invisible.

## Modeling boundary
Document effects that may require transmission-line models, more detailed conductors, openEMS/full-wave 3D work, or measurement.

## Deployment connection
Cable routing, conductive mast structure, bonding, and choke placement can become field-repeatability problems.

## Deliverable
Ideal baseline, representative feed comparison, current observations, pattern comparison, and a statement of what the simplified model can and cannot prove.
